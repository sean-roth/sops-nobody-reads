## 0. Goal and definition of done

The six-pass audit got the course to match its frozen sources. In doing so, regulatory precision migrated **up** into the learner-facing track — screen text now argues with the course's own build history and speaks reg-language to techs. This rework restores the dual-track design without regressing a single audit finding.

**Done means:**

1. No internal/production language visible to learners (e.g. "Movement B").
2. No "audit scar" negations — corrections aimed at errors only previous builds made ("not five," "not a repeat of the first one," "isn't a fourth role" as a two-slide teaching unit).
3. Module 1 and Module 3 restructured per §5 and §7; Module 2 light-touch per §6.
4. All 12 quiz questions rewritten to the spec in §9.
5. Landing page (`builds/index.html`) copy replaced per §10 — no compliance-outcome claims, no unoffered-service claims. **(Done.)**
6. **Gate A passes:** citation-only re-run of the audit tooling — every `source` block (slide-level and question-level) byte-identical to the pre-rework state, every anchor still resolving against frozen sources.
7. **Gate B passes:** cold learner read by a fresh Claude instance with zero project context, asked: Where does it repeat itself? Where does the pacing sag? Where would a maintenance tech tune out? Findings at "friction" or below only.

---

## 1. Governing principle

**Push precision down, not out.** Every fix follows one rule: regulatory precision lives in the `source` blocks (Reviewer toggle); plain speech lives on the screen (Learner view). When a slide's screen text is doing the anchor's job, compress the screen text and let the frozen anchor carry the letter of the standard. Nothing gets deleted from the course's evidentiary layer — it gets re-homed to the layer built for it.

This is not a softening pass. The voice stays direct ("A sign is a request. A lock is a fact."). What goes is reg-structure-as-screen-text and teaching-by-negation against our own drafts.

---

## 2. Hard constraints

- **Frozen anchors.** No `source` block may be edited — citation, URL, or anchor string. Slides may be rewritten, merged, moved, or cut *around* their anchors. Any slide that is cut or merged must have its `source` block re-homed to the surviving slide carrying that content. Gate A verifies this mechanically.
- **Scope fence.** Text and structure in `builds/module-0*.json` plus copy in `builds/index.html`. No image regeneration (image refs may move with their slides or be dropped from merged slides; no new image requirements). No player changes. No new modules. No script back-propagation (see §3, decision 1).
- **Doctrine exceptions (deliberate, approved):** Module 3 will carry two `list` slides (the split restoration sequence) — an approved deviation from the one-list-per-module cap, because two 4–5 item screens beat one 9-item wall. Recorded here so a future audit doesn't "fix" it back.

## 8. New slide spec: the notification map

The one addition (decision 4). The course currently teaches five notification moments in fragments across two modules and never lays them side by side — it was the audit's blocker area and it's the muddiest thread for a learner.

- **Type:** `concept` (or `options`-style grid if the player renders it cleaner — Engineer's call, content fixed).
- **Label:** "Who Gets Told, When"
- **Content:** five rows, each one line: (1) before locks go on — affected employees; (2) locks coming off, before restart — affected employees; (3) job complete, machine running — affected employees; (4) lock removed in your absence — you, before you return to any equipment; (5) shift change — the handoff *is* the notification.
- **No source block.** Every row's authority already lives on the slide that teaches it; the map consolidates, it doesn't cite. (Adding a fifth+ anchor here would require audit sign-off — out of scope.)
- **Placement:** end of movement B, per §7.

## 9. Quiz overhaul spec

All 12 questions rewritten. Structure preserved: 4 per module, ≥1 scenario question per module, questions follow content order, 75% passing.

**Rules:**

1. **Length parity.** No option longer than ~1.5× the mean length of the other three. The correct answer must never be identifiable by size. (Currently violated by M1 Q3, M3 Q2, M3 Q3.)
2. **Distractors are real beliefs.** Every wrong answer is something a working tech actually thinks — mine The Excuses list and the course's legitimate misconceptions: stop button = safe; the lead's lock covers the team; pressure bleeds off on its own; a tag holds like a lock; quick jobs are exempt; a supervisor's master key is the emergency plan; "qualified" means experienced. "Wait 15 minutes" (M2 Q3) is the existing gold standard — that's the bar.
3. **Determinate stems.** One defensibly correct answer per question. M1 Q3 specifically: stop testing attitude in a counting costume. Either the stem enumerates the machine's sources and the question tests the stored-energy shadow behind each, or it asks directly what step one exists to find out. Pick one; don't blend.
4. **Frozen question-level sources.** M1 Q3 and M3 Q4 carry `source` blocks — untouched. The rewritten questions must still be fully supported by those anchors; if a rewrite drifts from its anchor, the rewrite changes, never the anchor.
5. **Hygiene.** No catch-all options; correct answers distributed across positions (no position pattern across a module); scenario names may reuse Sarah/Dave for continuity.
