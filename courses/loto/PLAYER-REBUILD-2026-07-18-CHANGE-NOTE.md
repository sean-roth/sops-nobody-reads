## Flagged for Sean — not guessed

1. **"Why It Matters" (M1) and "The Whole Point" (M2).** Both are the course's own concluding rationale ("Simple is exactly what makes it dangerous," "Simple enough that there's no good reason not to do it") rather than a claim tracing directly to a single cited provision. §S2 reserves teaching-caption for atom types rule/threshold/definition/procedure-step and doesn't list rationale. They are clearly not beliefs-to-refute (misconception-held-up's job), so teaching-caption is the closer fit by elimination — but this is a genuine edge in the Standard's own gate that's worth a second look rather than silently deciding it.
2. **M1 "The Problem" and M3 "The Gap" (`concept`, untouched).** Both box a narrated complication ("He pressed the stop button... So he's working," "For the next four minutes, this machine is unprotected") rather than a sourced definition — `concept`'s Register I job per §S3. Structurally they read as the setup half of a scene→problem→reveal beat, closer to Register II scaffolding. Left as `concept` — not reclassified — because no clean existing container fits better without guessing, and the brief's reclassification instruction was scoped to `keypoint`.
3. **The Standard's own second motivating example has no held-up slide.** §S3 cites "the fourth-role belief" alongside the padlock-on-a-breaker as the two examples motivating `misconception-held-up`. The build's correction of that belief exists (M3's "A Different Standard" reveal: qualified person isn't a fourth LOTO role), but no slide currently states the belief itself for `misconception-held-up` to hold up — only the correction survived into the deck. Adding one would be new scaffolding content, out of scope for a type-only pass. Flagging for a follow-up content decision: accept the reveal alone, or add a held-up slide in a content pass.

## Chrome (§S4)

One tokenized shell, sourced from `sopsnobodyreads-site/styles.css`, shared by all three module players (generated from one template — see build notes below):

- Light default: `--bg` #f4ede0, `--ink` #1f3a2e, `--accent` #3a1f2e, `--ink-dim` #6b5d4a, fonts Lora (display/body) + IBM Plex Sans (UI).
- Dark toggle (in-session, not persisted — no localStorage, per the LMS-iframe constraint): `--bg` #141f19, `--ink` #f2ead9, `--accent` #e0a8c4, `--ink-dim` #b9ac95.
- Contrast checked by computed WCAG ratio, not assumed: ink/bg 10.6:1, ink-dim/bg 5.5:1, accent/bg 12.7:1 (light); ink/bg 14.0:1, ink-dim/bg 7.5:1, accent/bg 8.5:1 (dark). All clear AA (4.5:1) for normal text; most clear AAA.
- Font import widened beyond styles.css's exact weight list (added Lora 700, Plex Sans 500/600/700) — same two families, no new fonts, needed for register-weight separation (teaching-caption vs. misconception-held-up vs. UI labels).
- Imagery (industrial-noir art) is untouched (§S5). Because the art is inherently dark, any slide `has-image` renders its text on a fixed light-on-scrim scheme regardless of the light/dark toggle — the toggle governs the chrome and all non-image slides; the photo dictates its own local surface. Register weight (hero vs. quoted vs. UI label) holds identically either way; verified by screenshot in both toggle states.
- Register hierarchy is expressed through scale, weight, and containment (not color alone), so it survives both modes and would survive a client palette swap without redesign.
