### BLOCKER 1.1 — The restored notification step is sequenced after startup; (e)(2)(ii) mandates it before

**Frozen source it fails against:** (e)(2)(ii): "After lockout or tagout devices have been removed and **before a machine or equipment is started**, affected employees shall be notified that the lockout or tagout device(s) have been removed."

**Where it fails:**
- `builds/module-03.json` slide[3] (L21) — restoration step 7: "**Re-energize.** Follow the documented startup sequence." Step 8: "**Notify.** Tell affected employees the job is done."
- `scripts/module-03-…md` L61 — "**Re-energize.** Follow the manufacturer's startup procedures. Not the reverse of shutdown — the specific startup sequence documented for this equipment." Then L68: "**Notify affected employees that the job is done.** … the machine is back, **it's live**, and anyone who assumed it was still down needs to hear otherwise."
- `builds/module-03.json` slide[5] (L27) — "Notify affected employees the machine is back **and live**." Mirrored in the player inline data. The summary card (slide[32]) reasserts it: "notify affected employees **at the end**."

**Why BLOCKER:** the mandatory notification window is *after removal, before start*. The course's step 7, in its own words, executes "the manufacturer's startup sequence"; step 8 then notifies. As taught, notification lands after startup — outside the mandatory window, and less cautious than the source: the entire point of (e)(2)(ii) is that people learn the machine's state changed *before* it can run.

The defense, stated fairly: Appendix A's own model sequence is (4) "Remove the lockout devices and reenergize the machine or equipment," then (5) notify — and the course's anchor commentary explicitly says the step was "positioned last to match Appendix A's own model sequence." That defense works only if *reenergize* (restore energy supply) and *start* (operate) are distinct events — which is the only reading under which Appendix A is consistent with (e)(2)(ii), and consistent with Appendix A's own notify content ("ready **for use**," i.e., not yet in use). The course forecloses that reading itself: its step 7 collapses re-energization into the "startup sequence," and its notify slide teaches that the machine is already "live" when the notice goes out. A compliance-literate buyer gets the whole contradiction on one screen: slide[5] renders (e)(2)(ii) verbatim in its own Reviewer chip, directly beneath a body line that violates it.

Two things for the accountable signer, not resolved here: (a) the minimal fix is small — either notify between removal and startup, or split "re-energize" (restore supply) from "start" (operate) so notification demonstrably precedes operation; (b) the Appendix-A-ordering-vs-(e)(2)(ii) tension is a *within-source* conflict, and under Durability Standard §5 the choice between the two passages is a decision-record item either way — Pass-6 made the call silently inside an anchor comment.

## META — for the course-audit skill

1. **Sweep surfaces are path-scoped one directory too narrow.** The precondition and every drift sweep to date covers `scripts/` and `builds/`; `courses/loto/README.md` sits above both and carried "Five energy types," "emergency removal," and a phantom source document through three audits. Add course-folder front-matter (README, BRIEF, PRODUCTION-LOG) to every sweep, and to the precondition itself.
2. **G2 needs claim-level granularity, stated explicitly.** Pass-6 ran G2 at section-header level, self-reported clean, and missed two claim-level losses (2.1, 2.2) — one of which the fix then built a dangling reference on top of. The spec should say: trace *taught claims*, not sections; a section present in JSON proves nothing about the claims inside it.
3. **Fix passes inherit upstream flaws by design — the brief should say which ones ride along.** Fix 5 restored the script's (c)(6) content "matching the scripts' own treatment" and thereby promoted a flagged interpretation ("watches… perform it," 1.5) from script NOTE to learner-visible build claim. When a fix restores script content, the fix brief should enumerate open NOTEs on that span so restoring and re-shipping a known flaw is a decision, not a side effect.
4. **Within-source conflicts fit no pass.** Pass 1 checks course-vs-source; nothing checks source-vs-source.
