## Method and its limits

Verification was by source analysis and by recomputation from the committed
evidence artifacts — not by re-rendering. This environment has no browser
automation, so the `getBoundingClientRect()` figures and the screenshots could
not be independently reproduced. Where a claim rests on rendering I could not
repeat, that is stated rather than absorbed into a PASS.

Two things this bought that a screenshot pass would not have: the P2 cascade was
resolved from the stylesheet, and every contrast row was recomputed from its
stored sample pixels. Both turned up findings (P2-a, and the contrast-table
discrepancy).

**Coldness caveat.** Per the orchestrator's ruling, this ran in a session that
was not hermetically cold: the auditor had ambient access to Sean's profile, a
listing of memory-file names, and installed skill descriptions. No project file,
prior change note, or trace document was opened. Reading was confined to the
kickoff brief, the DEMO-POLISH brief, PR #85, the evidence branch, and the
commits named in the brief. On the substance of PR #85 the audit was cold; the
qualification is recorded rather than claimed away.

## P2 — chip pinned bottom-right; `> *` override no longer applies — **PASS** (positioning), with finding P2-a

*Method: full specificity survey of the merged stylesheet, per the kickoff
brief's instruction to verify by survey rather than by screenshot. Comments
stripped; all rules extracted, including inside `@media` blocks.*

Exactly three rules declare `position` and can match a `.citation-chip` inside
`.slide.has-image`:

| line | selector | specificity | declaration |
|---|---|---|---|
| 64 | `.slide.has-image > *` | (0,2,0) | `position: relative` |
| 791 | `.citation-chip` | (0,1,0) | `position: absolute` |
| **809** | **`.slide.has-image .citation-chip`** | **(0,3,0)** | **`position: absolute`** ← wins |

No `!important` anywhere in the file; no media-query rule overrides either
selector. The resolution therefore holds at every viewport — which is what makes
the check's "both widths" clause verifiable by survey rather than by capture.

This confirms the change note's correction of the brief's own math. The brief
described the fix as "same (0,2,0) specificity … wins the tie [by source order]."
The fix selector is three classes — **(0,3,0)** — and outranks line 64 outright;
no tie-break is involved. The builder caught this during planning, as the brief's
"confirm in plan" instruction was designed to force. The correction is right.

### Finding P2-a — the `> *` rule still applies, and still wins a declaration

The check's wording — "the `> *` override no longer applies to the chip" — is
literally false, and in a way that has a consequence nobody recorded.

`.slide.has-image > *` still **matches** the chip. Only its `position`
declaration was displaced. It also declares `z-index: 2`, and at (0,2,0) that
beats the base `.citation-chip` rule's own `z-index: 3` at (0,1,0). The fix
block at line 809 does not re-declare `z-index`. Net effect:

- on an **image** slide, the chip renders at **z-index 2**
- on a **plain** slide, the chip renders at **z-index 3**

The change note's Plan asserts the opposite: "`right`/`bottom`/`z-index` are
unaffected by this bug (they're not touched by the line-65 override)." That is
correct for `right`/`bottom` and incorrect for `z-index`.

**Assessed impact: low, and no observed defect.** The scrim
(`.slide.has-image::before`) is z-index 1, so the chip still paints above it.
Every sibling in the content stack also sits at z-index 2, so the chip is now
level with its siblings rather than above them, with overlap resolved by paint
order. With the chip pinned bottom-right and the content stack centred, no
overlap is expected, and the captured rects show none.

**Why it went unnoticed:** `getBoundingClientRect()` reports geometry, not
stacking. A rect-identity check — however rigorous, and this one was rigorous —
cannot see a z-index divergence. This is exactly the class of thing the kickoff
brief's "specificity survey, not just screenshot" instruction was aimed at, and
it is the one place that instruction paid for itself.

The positioning claim itself is sound by cascade analysis. The reported rect
identity between image and plain slides is consistent with it, but was not
independently reproduced (see Method).

**Clause 2 — confirmed on what was swept.** `full-deck-diff-report.json`: 184

audit's 30/27/35 per module sums to the same 92.

### Finding P4-a — the overflow gate is insensitive to this pass, and carries no provenance

**734 of 736 records have `scrollHeight == clientHeight` exactly.** The metric
reports the container box, which is pinned to the viewport unless content
actually overflows. Adding padding and a border to scene text could not have
moved these numbers unless it pushed a slide into overflow. So byte-identity here
is the *expected* result and carries little independent information about this
change — the gate correctly attests "no new clipping," and cannot attest more
than that.

Separately, the JSON has no run metadata — no tree SHA, no timestamp. A genuine
clean re-run and an un-rerun copy of the baseline are indistinguishable from the
artifact alone. That is not an accusation; the change note says it was re-run and
nothing contradicts that. It is a gap in what the artifact can prove.
**Recommend a run header (tree SHA + timestamp + tool version) on future audit JSON.**

### Finding P4-b — two surfaces were never swept

- **Reviewer mode.** No waypoint in the diff report references Reviewer mode or
  the chip; all 184 are Learner mode. The chip only renders in Reviewer mode, so
  a Learner-mode sweep provides *zero* coverage of the chip rule's blast radius
  across the deck. The chip was verified on module-01 slides 6 and 7 only. The
  change note argues one Learner sweep "already accounts for every
  unintended-change risk" — that holds for the scene rule, but not for the chip
  rule, whose entire effect is invisible in the mode that was swept.
- **Phone width.** All 184 waypoints are 1440×900. The scene rule's padding is
  viewport-relative (`2.5vh 3vw`), so its layout effect differs at 390. Only the
  four targeted scene slides were captured at that width.

Neither gap produced an observed defect. Both are unmeasured surface.

## Additional finding — the change note's contrast table does not match its cited evidence

*Method: recomputed every row from its stored `textRgb`/`worstCaseBg` samples
using WCAG relative luminance, and re-derived each threshold from the recorded
font size and weight.*

**The tooling is sound and its conclusion holds.** All 8 rows of
`contrast-report.json` recompute to their stored `contrastRatio` (Δ < 0.01);
every threshold is correctly derived (3.0 for ≥24px, else 4.5); all 8 pass, with
a minimum margin of **2.38** above threshold. AA on the shipped treatment is
independently verified. `contrast-check.js`'s technique — transparent-text
re-render, worst-case sampled pixel, per-viewport computed font size — is
correct, and the per-viewport threshold shift on the paragraph (3.0 at 1440,
4.5 at 390) is real, not an artifact.

**But six of eight rows in `CHANGE-NOTE-demo-polish.md` disagree with the artifact:**

No verdict changes — every row passes comfortably under either set. The 1440

Also confirmed from the artifact, and consistent with the shipped CSS: the label
