## BLOCKERS — contradicts the source or is less cautious than it

### B1 — "Five types" attributed to OSHA; thermal energy omitted
**Where:** Module 1 — "Five Types" section, convergence diagram spec, end card, Quiz Q3.
**Course claims:** "OSHA's Control of Hazardous Energy standard … recognizes five types" (electrical, hydraulic/pneumatic, mechanical, gravitational, chemical).
**Source, 1910.147(b):** "*Energy source.* Any source of electrical, mechanical, hydraulic, pneumatic, chemical, thermal, or other energy."
**Defects:** (1) Attribution false — the regulation's list has six named types plus "other"; it does not name gravitational. (2) **Thermal is absent from the course entirely** (steam gets one mention filed under chemical). Less cautious by omission; target audience includes food processing (ovens, steam, hot surfaces). An OSHA interpretation letter (2000-04-11, Dykes) treats natural gas heating an oven as an energy source requiring an energy control procedure on thermal-hazard grounds.
**Fix direction:** Quote the regulation's definition accurately; present the course grouping explicitly as the course's teaching frame, not OSHA's taxonomy. Add thermal as a taught type (steam lines, ovens, hot surfaces — food-processing native). Keep gravitational but frame as stored energy in elevated loads, not as a regulatory category. Propagate to diagram spec, end card, Quiz Q3.

### B2 — "Four types of employees" — the standard defines three; "Qualified" is Subpart S
**Where:** Module 3 — "Who Does What" section, concentric-circles visual spec, Quiz Q4 narration context.
**Course claims:** "The OSHA standard defines four types of employees … Authorized, Affected, Qualified, Other."
**Source:** 1910.147(b) defines *authorized* and *affected*; (c)(7)(i)(C) adds *all other employees*. "Qualified" belongs to Subpart S (electrical safety-related work practices) — and 1910.147(a)(1)(ii)(D) **explicitly excludes** Subpart S electric-utilization work from this standard's scope.
**Fix direction:** Teach three roles as this standard's roles. Optional one-liner preserving the useful term: "You may also hear 'qualified person' — that's a different standard (electrical work, Subpart S), outside this course." Quiz Q4's "qualified employee" may remain as a distractor once the narration stops teaching it as part of 1910.147.
**Keep:** "An affected employee can become an authorized employee when their duties change" — nearly verbatim from (b). Anchored.

### B3 — Stored-energy reaccumulation never taught
**Where:** Module 2 — Step Five; Module 2 end card.
**Source, 1910.147(d)(5)(ii):** "If there is a possibility of reaccumulation of stored energy to a hazardous level, verification of isolation shall be continued until the servicing or maintenance is completed, or until the possibility of such accumulation no longer exists."
**Defect:** The course teaches (d)(5)(i) (relieve, block, discharge) well and omits (d)(5)(ii) entirely. Pneumatic systems re-pressurize; capacitors recharge; hydraulic pressure creeps. Step five as taught is less protective than the source's step five. Mandatory sequence element, zero coverage.
**Fix direction:** Surgical insert in Step Five narration + one end-card line: if energy can build back, verification continues until the job is done.

## FRICTION — fix before showing anyone who knows the standard

- **F1 — Tagout structure garbled.** Course: tagout "only acceptable when the machine physically cannot accommodate a lock — and even then, the employer must prove … equivalent protection." Source: non-lockable device → tagout system required, (c)(2)(i); the full-protection demonstration, (c)(3), attaches to the opposite case — tagout used on a device *capable* of being locked out, (c)(2)(ii). Course is more restrictive than source (survives conservative-bias gate) but misstates the structure under an "OSHA's standard is clear" banner. Restate accurately.
- **F2 — Internal contradiction on lock removal.** M2: "No one removes someone else's lock. Ever." M3 then correctly teaches the (e)(3) employer-directed exception. Add one qualifier in M2 pointing to the M3 procedure.
- **F3 — "Master keys are prohibited" stated as regulatory fact.** No such prohibition in the fetched text. Reframe as program-design principle flowing from exclusive control, or add a supporting interpretation letter as an accepted source.
- **F4 — Emergency removal missing its container.** The three steps taught match (e)(3)(i)–(iii); missing: removal happens *under the direction of the employer* per a *pre-documented procedure incorporated into the energy control program*. Add the container sentence.
- **F5 — Bad number.** "2000 PSI … over a hundred times the pressure in your car tires." At 30–36 PSI, ≈55–65×. Fix to "roughly sixty times." Credibility detail aimed at an audience that knows tire pressures.
- **F6 — "Contractor certification."** (f)(2) requires mutual information exchange between on-site and outside employers; it creates no certification artifact. Rename/reframe in the Paperwork section.
